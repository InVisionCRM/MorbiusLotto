Mobile UI exploration · 2026-05-27

Three ways to make mobile
poker actually playable.

The current landscape mobile layout dedicates ~55% of the screen to chrome. Industry leaders give the table 75–85%. Below are three directions — each rivals a different competitor, each makes a different tradeoff.

01 · What's wrong today

Eight competing surfaces, one tiny table

Counting every distinct UI region in the screenshot you sent: the table is fighting eight other elements for attention. On a real phone this becomes unplayable because hit targets shrink, the felt gets cropped, and key info (stack size, pot odds) becomes hard to read at a glance.

01

Promo banner

"Promote your token here for 50 MORBIUS" eats a full row at the top. Unrelated to the hand in progress.

02

Token info row

The MORBIUS / $MORBIUS row + the SCAN.MORBIUS.IO chip are second-tier info that doesn't need to live above the action bar.

03

Left stats column

BLINDS / STACK / RANK / LEFT in a stacked column takes ~80px of horizontal real estate that the table needs.

04

Activity rail

Vertical "ACTIVITY" tab on the right pinches the table further. On mobile it should slide in only when summoned.

05

Pre-action column

Check/Fold · Check · Call Any always visible — three checkboxes that only matter for ~1s before your turn.

06

Bet preset row

Min · ½ Pot · Pot · All-in always visible above the action bar. They only matter while you're raising.

07

Slider + ± buttons

A slider, a ± stepper, and the Raise button all crammed into the right half of the action bar.

08

Tiny action buttons

Fold / Check / Call buttons are only ~50px tall and ~25% of width. Industry standard is ~60–70px tall, ~30% width.

02 · Design principles

What PokerStars, GG, and WPT do right

Every leading mobile poker app converges on these five rules. They're not optional — they're the floor.

1

Table first

The felt fills 75–85% of the screen. Everything else either overlays it temporarily or hides behind a tap.

2

Three primary actions

Fold · Check/Call · Bet/Raise. Always. Big, color-coded, thumb-reachable. No 4th button.

3

Sheets for sizing

Bet sizing lives in a bottom sheet that opens on Raise tap. Slider, presets, and chips all together — gone when not needed.

4

Pre-actions tucked away

Auto-fold / Check / Call Any live behind a small icon toggle. Power users find them; new users aren't distracted.

5

Glanceable status

Blinds, level, stack, and rank live in a single collapsed pill at the top. Tap to expand.

Refinements · v1.1

Three things we just fixed

Based on the feedback: avatars need to earn their space, chat/activity needs a home, and the action badge needs to live inside the player tile — not float above it.

Q1

Use the avatars we already have. Right call.

You already have AvatarView — a procedural SVG system with face shapes, hair, eyes, hats, glasses, mouth props, and ~30 emotions including a dedicated poker emotion. It tracks the mouse for the current player, roams eyes for idle players, and sleeps for sitting-out players. PokerSeat.tsx already renders it.

You also already have hideSeatAvatar on PokerSeat — a prop you wrote for narrow viewports. The real question isn't what the avatars look like, it's at what tile size does the full AvatarView stop earning its space. Below: the same character rendered at four sizes that exist on mobile.

Recommendation

At 34px on the seat tiles in Concept 1, the full AvatarView is borderline — features are there but micro-detailed work (mouth props, fine hair, makeup) is wasted. Use the existing hideSeatAvatar path on mobile, fall back to letter glyph with the player's avatar accent color extracted from their config — cheap, recognizable, and your existing code path. Render the full AvatarView at 48px+ in the hero zone (Concept 2 portrait) and at 72–96px in the opponent profile card when a player is tapped. That's where the procedural detail and emotions actually pay off.

Q2

Where are the chat / activity feed buttons?

One icon, top-right of the action bar header, with unread badge. Tap it to open a side drawer that slides in from the right. The drawer has tabs for Chat and Activity — no need for two permanent buttons. Closes by tapping outside or swiping right.

03 · Side by side

Pick your tradeoff

The three concepts solve different problems. Use this table to decide which one matches your priorities right now.

Visual polish ceiling High High Highest

⚡ My pick

Ship Concept 01 now. Plan Concept 02 next quarter.

Concept 01 (Compact Horizontal) is the right immediate move. It keeps your existing component architecture and orientation, but ruthlessly removes the chrome that's killing mobile playability today. Most of the value is just hiding what shouldn't be visible — the promo banner, the token row, the bet presets, the pre-action checkboxes. That's a 1–2 week fix.

Concept 02 (Portrait First) is the right strategic move for the next quarter. Every leading mobile poker app is portrait-first because that's how people use their phones. It's a bigger build because you're effectively maintaining a second layout, but the payoff is a casual-friendly experience that competes directly with PokerStars Mobile and GG Poker. Consider it the v2.

Concept 03 (Premium Minimal) is a visual treatment, not a separate concept. The floating glass surfaces, chip-stack bet visualization, and pot-odds display can be layered onto either 01 or 02. Treat it as a polish pass you apply once the foundation lands.

Suggested rollout: Sprint 1 — ship Concept 01 to remove the bleeding. Sprint 2 — start Concept 02 in portrait, gated to mobile-only screens behind a feature flag. Sprint 3 — apply Concept 03 polish to both. The current desktop layout doesn't change.
