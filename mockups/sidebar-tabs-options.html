Sidebar redesign · 3 options

Show stats without breaking the hand

All three keep the player able to act the moment it's their turn — no modal in the way. Each one has a real tradeoff. Pick the one that fits the room you want to leave for chat and the activity feed.

Tabs replace the activity feed

Stats tab is open — Activity tab badges to grab attention when your turn comes.

Pros: Cleanest tabbed UX. Stats tab gets the full sidebar height — chart, stat grid, and a detail list all fit.
Cons: Activity feed is hidden while another tab is active. The red pulsing badge on the Activity tab partly mitigates that, plus we can auto-switch back to Activity the instant the server prompts you to act (so the action UI is anchored next to a visible feed).
Effort: ≈4–6 hrs. New tab state in the sidebar, four panels extracted from existing modals.

Split sidebar — activity always visible

Top half stays the live feed + chat. Bottom half is a tabbed panel for stats/settings/help.

Pros: You never lose sight of the feed. No "did I miss my turn" anxiety, no auto-switch logic. Most "trader station" feel.
Cons: Both panels share the vertical space, so stats and feed are each ~half height. On short laptop screens (≤720px) it gets tight. The bottom panel can collapse down to just the tab strip when not needed, restoring full feed height.
Effort: ≈6–8 hrs. Two scrollable regions, the divider, and the same panel extractions as A.

Pros: Most powerful — open Stats and Settings simultaneously, drag either out of the way of the cards, pin/persist position across sessions. The activity sidebar stays clean and focused on chat/actions.
Cons: Drag-resize-collapse-persist logic adds real complexity. Mobile-hostile (no point dragging on a 5-inch screen — would need a fallback mode that pins them in place, defeating most of the upside). Visual chrome competes with the table for attention.
Effort: ≈2 days. Drag-and-drop library (react-rnd or hand-rolled), z-index + position persistence in localStorage, mobile fallback path.
When to pick this: if you imagine future panels — equity calculator, hand replayer, mini-chart, voice-control HUD — that benefit from being arrangeable. If it's just Stats + Settings, B is plenty.
My pick is B for the first iteration — best UX-to-effort ratio and the easiest to evolve into C later if you want it.
